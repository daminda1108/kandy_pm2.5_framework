# Estimating urban air quality without local monitoring {#ch-weather-known-air}

Exposure to fine particulate matter is among the largest environmental contributors to mortality
and morbidity worldwide [@Burnett2018; @GBD2021Risk], and unlike many environmental hazards its
sources can be addressed through transport, fuel and land-use policy. Designing and evaluating
such policy requires knowledge of the concentration field over a city. In most cities of low- and
middle-income countries that field is not observed but estimated, usually from global models and
satellite-derived products, and few local observations exist against which the estimate can be
checked. {{This:ch-weather-known-air}} sets out why surface PM2.5 is harder to estimate than the meteorological
state, using numerical weather prediction as a point of reference, because the comparison
identifies which components of the problem limit accuracy.
