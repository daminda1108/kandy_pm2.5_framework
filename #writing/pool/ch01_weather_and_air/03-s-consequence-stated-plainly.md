## Validation where monitoring is absent {#s-consequence-stated-plainly}

For many cities, and for most cities in low- and middle-income countries, the available PM2.5
field is a model output that cannot be evaluated against local observations [@Martin2019].

The literature on satellite and reanalysis fusion products is extensive, and these products
report out-of-sample performance carefully [@vanDonkelaar2021; @Hammer2020; @Di2019; @Wei2023].
Out-of-sample evaluation, however, can only be conducted where monitors exist. A model trained
where monitoring is dense and applied where it is absent is therefore evaluated on one population
of locations and used on another, and the transfer of interest, from densely to sparsely
monitored settings, is the one that cannot be scored directly.

This is a structural property of the problem rather than a shortcoming of any particular product.
Its consequence is that the usual response to uncertainty about a model, more extensive testing,
is unavailable, because such testing requires the observations whose absence is the source of
the uncertainty.
