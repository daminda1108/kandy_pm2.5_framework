## Scope of the claims {#s-scope-claims}

Stated here rather than left for a reader to extract.

This thesis does not claim a validated neighbourhood-scale map of Kandy. {{ref:ch-model-stops}} shows that
such a map is not available at the resolution used, and explains why the limit is one of
definition, not of data. It does not claim that the model captures atmospheric chemistry,
because the model contains none. What is validated is narrower than the problem it is aimed at:
the construction is scored on a panel of monitored valley and basin cities, and applied to a
monitorless one by analogy. The target is by construction the kind of city the validation set
excludes, so the transfer is an argument from resemblance and not a theorem about
transportability. It does not claim that the construction is validated for coastal cities,
because every city in that model panel is a valley or a basin. The measurement of information
value uses separate and larger panels, which were selected on data availability with no terrain
criterion; the registered one is mostly temperate and mostly served by regulatory monitors, while
the target is tropical and has only low-cost sensors, so the measurement priorities transfer to it
with the same caveat. It does not claim that the share of concentration assigned to the local
increment is a source apportionment: it is a bound set by a constraint. And it does not claim
that machine learning solved the problem. The learning in this work is standard and the physics
is deliberately modest. What is new is the accounting: a declaration of what the model is
entitled to use, a guarantee that removing an entitlement degrades the model exactly, and a
measurement of what each entitlement is worth, reported with a design flaw in one registered
comparison that was found after the comparison had been run.
