## Physical setting {#s-city-hole}

Kandy lies in a steep-sided valley in the central highlands of Sri Lanka, about 500 metres above
sea level and about {{claim:donor.colombo_km}} kilometres east-north-east of Colombo in a straight
line. {{dia:valley}} shows the setting from the elevation model used throughout this work. The
basin is closed to the south by the Hantana range, which rises to between about 1,200 and 1,300
metres (1,297 metres in the elevation model used here) some seven kilometres south of the city
centre, and on its west, north and east sides the Mahaweli river loops around the city within
about one and a half to four kilometres of the centre. Relief across the modelled domain is
{{claim:kandy.relief_m}} metres over fifteen kilometres.

{{dia:valley}}

Compared with a city of similar size on flat terrain, this topography complicates the estimation
of PM2.5 in three respects.

First, concentrations vary over distances shorter than global products resolve. Terrain modifies
the flow near the valley floor relative to that represented in coarse meteorological fields, and
a single one-kilometre cell may contain a major junction, a river, a wooded slope and residential
areas, each with a different concentration.

Second, the relationship between emission and concentration depends on the boundary layer, which
coarse models represent only in aggregate. Nocturnal inversions and cold-air pooling can hold a
shallow polluted layer over a valley city for extended periods [@Whiteman2000; @Chemel2016], so
that the same emission produces very different concentrations under a mixed layer two hundred
metres deep and one two thousand metres deep.

Third, most monitoring in the basin is on or near the valley floor, where activity is
concentrated. A network sited in this way samples one level of a vertically stratified field and
does not constrain the remainder. This follows from the siting logic itself rather than from any
deficiency of a particular network.
