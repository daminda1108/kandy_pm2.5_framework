## Existing measurement record {#s-existing-measurement-record}

At the time of this study, the publicly available PM2.5 record for Kandy consisted of two
low-cost sensors, and no reference-grade record was available.

The city previously had a reference-grade instrument at Torrington Park, which anchored the
calibration of published low-cost sensor records in the region [@Attanayake2025]; it is no longer
in operation. The national environmental authority operates a monitoring station with an hourly
record extending back to 2019, with a gap from July 2021 to October 2022 when it could not be
operated (Central Environmental Authority, personal communication, 12 August 2026). That record
is not publicly released, and access requires a formal agreement.

The study area is therefore a city of 98,828 residents within its municipal boundary
[@DCS2012Census], into which nearly 389,000 commuters, more than twice that number, travel on a
typical weekday [@WorldBank2020KMTT], so that most of the people exposed to its air during the
day do not live in it. It has complex terrain and a monitoring record limited to two instruments
of a class with known calibration difficulties [@Morawska2018]. Beyond these, the independent
evidence consists of published annual and daily summaries, reviewed in
{{ref:s-two-decades-measurement}}, rather than a continuous record.

This situation is common rather than exceptional. Among cities worldwide with ten or more
concurrent reference monitors, the deep tropical band has {{claim:census.deep_tropical}} and the
temperate band has {{claim:census.temperate}}, a factor of
{{claim:census.temperate_over_deep_tropical}}. The climatic regime in which a method requiring no
local monitoring is most needed is therefore the one in which local monitoring is scarcest. Any
validation panel assembled for this problem inherits the imbalance, and for this reason every
result in {{ref:s-marginal-predictive-value-each}} is reported stratified by latitude band.
