## Limitations of the existing record {#s-gap-stated-list}

None of the studies above provides a continuous field over the city. Taken together, they cannot
supply the following.

*Concentrations at unsampled locations.* Every measurement was made at a site chosen either for
contrast, as in the roadside survey, or for institutional convenience, as with the fixed
stations. Neither sampling design supports interpolation to locations that were not sampled.

*A continuous record.* Each particulate campaign sampled a site for hours. The longest published
daily PM2.5 series covers two years at one location [@Nirmani2025], and the public low-cost
records, though longer, are not calibrated at Kandy. No record supports statements about a
particular hour on a particular day, which is the resolution at which exposure varies and at
which an episode would need to be recognised.

*A separation of local from regional contributions.* The quantity most relevant to local policy
is the division between what Kandy generates and what arrives from outside it, and no measurement
at one point can separate the two. The monsoon result of Abeyratne and Ileperuma is suggestive,
but it concerns gases rather than particulate mass and establishes a seasonal pattern rather than
a partition.

*Current data.* The Kandy reference monitor that anchored one of the published calibrations
[@Attanayake2025] is no longer operating, and a discontinued record cannot inform current
decisions.
