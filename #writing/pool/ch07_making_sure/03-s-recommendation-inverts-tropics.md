## The deep-tropical comparison, retracted as a finding {#s-recommendation-inverts-tropics}

Earlier versions of this thesis reported that the ordering of observations reverses in the deep
tropics, the band Kandy belongs to: that there, two local sensors were worth several times as much
as a background series, with a paired interval that excluded zero, and that a programme in Kandy
should therefore buy a local observation first. That result is retracted as a finding. It is
recorded here because it was the thesis's most quoted recommendation, and because the reasons it
failed are part of what the measurement established.

**It was one draw.** The result came from one station split and one learner seed. Repeated over
twenty alternative splits, the interval excluded zero in only three [ledger F.115]. Under the
redesigned ladder, which averages over twenty-one splits and five seeds before comparing anything,
the direction on the discovery panel still favoured the local stations, but the interval included
zero, and in the prospective arm the difference nearly vanished [ledger F.116]. On the exceedance
loss the background led even in the deep tropics.

**The confirmation cannot see it.** The registered moderator, the slope of the ordering on absolute
latitude, is undetectable ({{ref:s-registered-confirmation}}), with four scored cities below the
Tropic of Cancer. Public archives hold too few dense tropical networks to test the band directly:
across the whole open catalogue, {{claim:census.deep_tropical}} deep-tropical clusters hold ten or
more concurrent reference stations, against {{claim:census.temperate}} in temperate latitudes
({{ref:ch-kandy-setting-record-stakes}}).

**The comparison itself compared uses.** Like the registered ordering, the deep-tropical comparison
set a recalibration from two stations against a background read on the day
({{ref:s-like-for-like}}). Even its direction is therefore a statement about the rungs as built, and
used alike, stations of different kinds were worth the same on the panel as a whole.

**What remains** is a direction on about a dozen discovery cities, exploratory, and sensitive to the
satellite stream, the loss, the driver set, the station split, the learner seed and the use of the
stations. It is not quoted as a magnitude anywhere in this thesis.

### Why latitude would in any case be a label {#s-latitude-label}

Even a confirmed band difference would not have shown that latitude causes it. Band travels with at
least six other things. The first is instrument class: the deep-tropical cities of the panel are
mostly low-cost networks, while the other bands are mostly regulatory ({{ref:s-three-confounds-pooled-numbers}}).
The others are network density and design, driver completeness, the seasonal structure of the
meteorology, the source mix, and the institutional history that decides which cities publish data at
all.

A mechanism was proposed, and its status is an untested idea. In the deep tropics the seasonal cycle
of the regional background is weak, so a background series could add little that a model without
sensors has not already taken from reanalysis and geography, while in temperate latitudes a strong
winter accumulation regime would make the same series carry a great deal. If that is right, the
operative variable is the amplitude of the regional seasonal cycle and latitude stands in for it.
Testing it would require sorting cities by that amplitude directly, which {{ref:s-measurement-would-settle-most}}
lists and this thesis did not run.

For Kandy, the consequence is that no band-specific ordering of local stations against a background
is available, and none is needed. The result that does transfer is the one that holds for every kind
of station the panel contains: a reading used on the day is worth far more than a recalibration, so a
station of either kind whose readings reach the estimate daily is the priority, and a local station
and a regional background are complementary rather than ranked.
