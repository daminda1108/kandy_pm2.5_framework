## At matched support, the gap is small but real {#s-matched-support-model-close}

The apparent catastrophe of {{ref:s-observation-sets-problem}} is a comparison between quantities defined at different
supports. At matched support and matched averaging window the picture changes.

At monthly averaging the model's between-cell ratio is {{claim:field.contrast_monthly}}, against
1.26 to 1.51 measured across the stations of three panel cities with dense networks at the same
window [ledger F.76]. Annually the model gives {{claim:kandy.annual_contrast}}. Set beside the
eighty-five-fold spread of the kerbside survey, the model is of the right order.

{{fig:scales}}

It is not inside the observed range, however, and the figure shows a second difference that the
monthly comparison hides. In each of the three monitored cities the contrast between stations
falls as the averaging window lengthens, because short-lived local peaks average out. The
model's contrast does the opposite: it is lowest at the hourly window and rises slowly with the
window. The model is therefore most under-contrasted at the hourly scale, where it gives
{{claim:field.contrast_hourly}} against observed values between 1.8 and 2.5 [ledger F.76]. That follows from its
construction, since on well-ventilated hours the local increment that carries the pattern is
small, and it means the delivered field understates how much the air differs across the city
from hour to hour.

One mismatch remains and it is stated rather than allowed to flatter the agreement. The observed
figures are spreads across a city's **stations**; the model's is a spread across its **cells**.
Matching the averaging window removes the larger part of the discrepancy, and an earlier version
of this comparison set an annual model contrast against observed values taken at mixed windows,
which overstated the agreement. The residual is a support difference that cannot be closed
without a campaign designed for it.
