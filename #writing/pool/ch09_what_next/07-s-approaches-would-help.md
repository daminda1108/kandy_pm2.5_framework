## Approaches that would not help {#s-approaches-would-help}

Stated because these are the proposals most likely to be made.

**A larger model.** A model can only redistribute the information present in its inputs
({{ref:s-capabilities-limits-modelling}}), and the comparison of model families in
{{ref:s-six-negative-results-their}} found no family that recovered structure the inputs did not
encode.

A finer grid, for the reason given in {{ref:s-implications-radius-result}}.

More monitors in the same city beyond the first two or three that report daily, if the purpose
is the daily city level. {{ref:s-measurement-priority-ordering}} shows that the daily curve
flattens after a few stations read on the day. If the purpose is a map, more stations are the
relevant instrument, but {{ref:s-what-stations-buy-map}} finds that three to eight of them do not
rank neighbourhoods usefully, and that a satellite surface does no better. Siting them
deliberately across land-use contrast does not change that: {{ref:s-deliberate-siting-tested-dense}}
finds no paired advantage for deliberate siting on the panel's dense networks, and the spatial
learning curve finds none under a fixed held-out design.

More cities of the kind the panel already holds. The confirmation was mainly temperate and
regulatory, and adding more such cities would narrow intervals on questions already answered.
What would help is dense tropical networks, and {{ref:ch-kandy-setting-record-stakes}} established
that the cities which currently publish data do not include enough of them.
