## Aim and objectives {#a-aims}

The aim of this study is to reconstruct fine particulate matter over Kandy at hourly and
one-kilometre resolution from observations that exist everywhere, to establish what that
reconstruction can and cannot be trusted to say given the few observations the city has, and to
identify the observations that would most improve it.

Five objectives follow from that aim.

1. To construct a background-and-increment decomposition of fine particulate concentration for
   the Kandy basin that preserves the city-wide mean by construction, to within {{claim:gauge.drift_hi_pct}} per cent in practice, and declares which observations each
   component is permitted to use ({{ref:a-methods}}).
2. To characterise the reconstructed field: its level, its seasonal and daily cycles, its
   episodes, its spatial contrast, the share of it assigned to the local increment and the bounds
   on that share, and the population exposure it implies ({{ref:s-kandy-field}}).
3. To test the field against every independent record available at Kandy, and, where the
   construction cannot be tested at Kandy, to test it on monitored cities of the same kind by
   withholding their own observations ({{ref:s-checks-kandy-carry-weight}} and
   {{ref:ch-validation-without-local-ground}}).
4. To determine the spatial scale below which the field is no longer supported, the reason, and
   whether a small number of stations would change it ({{ref:ch-model-stops}}).
5. To identify which observations, of which kind, in what number and used in what way, would most
   improve the field at Kandy, from a measurement of what each observation is worth across a
   panel of monitored cities ({{ref:s-measurement-priority-ordering}} and {{ref:a-app-panel}}).
