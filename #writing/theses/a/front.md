# Abstract {.front}

::: {custom-style="Abstract Text"}
Kandy, a valley city in Sri Lanka's central highlands with about 99,000 residents and nearly 389,000 weekday commuters, has one regulatory monitor for fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and two low-cost sensors. This study builds a constrained hourly one-kilometre reconstruction of Kandy's PM2.5 for 2019 to 2023 from free satellite, reanalysis and geographic data, and tests how far each part holds. The field is a uniform regional background plus a local increment carrying all spatial structure, and its city-wide annual mean equals the temporal estimate by construction. Against the city-centre regulatory station the field follows the day-to-day sequence (daily correlation {{claim:cea.del.all.r}}, {{claim:cea.del.anomaly_daily_r}} within the month) and the timing of the daily cycle; with that cycle refitted to the station's hours before mid-2021, its night-to-midday ratio on later hours is {{claim:cea.hourlyho.production.night_over_midday}} against the station's {{claim:cea.hourlyho.station.night_over_midday}}. The station showed the satellite-derived level {{claim:cea.sat.all.bias_pct}} per cent high; re-anchored on 2019 to 2021, the field is within {{claim:ceasplit.bias_re}} per cent on held-out days ({{claim:cea.del.2022.bias_pct}} per cent in 2022, +{{claim:cea.del.2023.bias_pct}} per cent in 2023), a provisional one-station correction. Annual means of {{claim:kandy.mean_min}} to {{claim:kandy.mean_max}} micrograms per cubic metre exceed the World Health Organization guideline every year, by at least {{claim:ceabeta.who_lo}} times across the calibration interval. In {{claim:scorecard.cities}} monitored cities scored on withheld stations, the seasonal and daily cycles are reproduced about as well as by their two anchor stations' own climatology and far better than by the free composition prior. Across {{claim:v2.km.cities_scored}} cities, the deployed temporal model reduces daily error by {{claim:v2.km.reco.unio.gK2_rmse.median}} per cent against a sensorless estimate retrospectively, but by {{claim:v2.km.pros.unio.gK2_rmse.median}} per cent ({{claim:v2.km.pros.unio.gK2_rmse.lo}} to {{claim:v2.km.pros.unio.gK2_rmse.hi}}) prospectively, not an established improvement. Two stations read daily cut error by {{claim:v2.review.k.day2.median}} per cent, against {{claim:v2.review.k.cal2.median}} when they only calibrate the estimate. The spatial pattern is a hypothesis, not a validated neighbourhood map, and the local increment, {{claim:partition.f}} of the total, is a property of the decomposition rather than a source apportionment. For Kandy this favours daily-read stations and a network built to test, not fit, the field.
