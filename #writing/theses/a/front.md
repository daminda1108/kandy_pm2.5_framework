# Abstract {.front}

::: {custom-style="Abstract Text"}
Kandy, a valley city in Sri Lanka's central highlands with about 99,000 residents and nearly 389,000 weekday commuters, has no publicly reporting reference monitor for fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and two low-cost sensors. This study builds a constrained reconstruction of PM2.5 over the Kandy basin, hourly at one kilometre for 2019 to 2023, from free satellite, reanalysis and geographic data, and establishes how far each part can be trusted. The field is a uniform regional background plus a local increment carrying all spatial structure; the city-wide mean is conserved to within {{claim:gauge.drift_hi_pct}} per cent. The modelled annual means, {{claim:kandy.mean_min}} to {{claim:kandy.mean_max}} micrograms per cubic metre, exceed the World Health Organization guideline every year, and remain at least {{claim:lvl.tropical.who_lo}} times it if the satellite level errs as it does in tropical cities. The evidence is graded. The seasonal cycle transfers to all {{claim:scorecard.cities}} monitored valley cities scored on withheld stations and matches a national daily record at Kandy across calendar months (correlation {{claim:nbrod.climatology_r}}); day-to-day agreement there is weak ({{claim:nbrod.anomaly_daily_r}}). Across {{claim:v2.km.cities_scored}} cities, the deployed temporal model reduces daily error by {{claim:v2.km.reco.unio.gK2_rmse.median}} per cent against a generic sensorless estimate retrospectively, over periods its two anchor stations observed, but by no resolvable amount prospectively. The absolute level is open: the national record sits above the field, three low-cost records below. The spatial pattern is a hypothesis, not a validated neighbourhood map, and a dispersion step that lowered agreement with held-out stations was removed. The local increment is {{claim:partition.f}} of the modelled total at baseline ({{claim:v2.f.cap_min}} to {{claim:v2.f.cap_max}} across specification choices), not a source apportionment. Measured across cities, one station read daily reduces daily error by {{claim:v2.review.k.day1.median}} per cent and two by {{claim:v2.review.k.day2.median}} per cent, against {{claim:v2.review.k.cal2.median}} per cent when the same stations only calibrate the estimate; no ordering of station types is established. For Kandy this favours continuously reporting stations read daily, with a reference-grade instrument to settle the level.
