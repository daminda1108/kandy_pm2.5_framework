# Abstract {.front}

::: {custom-style="Abstract Text"}
Kandy, a valley city in Sri Lanka's central highlands with about 99,000 residents and nearly 389,000 weekday commuters, has no publicly reporting reference monitor for fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and two low-cost sensors. This study builds a constrained reconstruction of PM2.5 over the Kandy basin, hourly at one kilometre for 2019 to 2023, from free satellite, reanalysis and geographic data, and establishes how far each part can be trusted. The field is a uniform regional background plus a local increment carrying all spatial structure; the city-wide mean is conserved to within {{claim:gauge.drift_hi_pct}} per cent. The modelled annual basin means, {{claim:kandy.mean_min}} to {{claim:kandy.mean_max}} micrograms per cubic metre, exceed the World Health Organization guideline every year. Without local ground truth, the field is validated three ways, and the evidence is graded. The seasonal cycle transfers to all {{claim:scorecard.cities}} monitored valley and basin cities scored by withholding their own observations. On a cross-city ladder of information budgets in {{claim:v2.km.cities_scored}} cities, the deployed temporal model reduces daily error by {{claim:v2.km.reco.unio.gK2_rmse.median}} per cent against a generic sensorless estimate where its two anchor stations observed the period, but not where they did not. The absolute level remains open: a national record at Kandy agrees with the field, three low-cost records sit below it. The spatial pattern is a hypothesis, not a validated neighbourhood map: more within-city variation lies inside a cell than between cells, and no free covariate places it. Under the baseline decomposition the local increment is {{claim:partition.f}} of the modelled total ({{claim:v2.f.cap_min}} to {{claim:v2.f.cap_max}} across specification choices, {{claim:field.f_form_roll48}} with a 48-hour background window), a decomposition property, not a source apportionment. The ladder also prices observations. One station read daily reduces daily error by {{claim:v2.review.k.day1.median}} per cent and two by {{claim:v2.review.k.day2.median}} per cent, against {{claim:v2.review.k.cal2.median}} per cent when the same stations only calibrate the estimate; the current evidence establishes no ordering of station types. For Kandy this favours continuously reporting stations read daily, with a reference-grade instrument among them to settle the level.
:::
