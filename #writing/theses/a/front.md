# Abstract {.front}

::: {custom-style="Abstract Text"}
Kandy, a valley city in the central highlands of Sri Lanka with about 99,000 residents and nearly 389,000 weekday commuters, has no working reference monitor for fine particulate matter (PM2.5) and two low-cost sensors. This study reconstructs PM2.5 over the Kandy basin at hourly and one-kilometre resolution for 2019 to 2023 from satellite retrievals, reanalysis meteorology and freely available geography, and asks what such a field can be trusted to say in a city that cannot check it. The field is built as a regional background, uniform across the city at any hour, plus a locally generated increment that carries all spatial structure and whose city-wide average is fixed, so that an error in the pattern moves material to the wrong place without creating any. The annual basin mean runs from {{claim:kandy.mean_min}} to {{claim:kandy.mean_max}} micrograms per cubic metre across the anchored years, above the World Health Organization guideline throughout, with a north-east monsoon maximum, a south-west monsoon minimum, and morning and evening peaks above a midday trough. Under stated assumptions the decomposition assigns {{claim:partition.f}} of the concentration to the local increment; this is a constrained split and not a source apportionment. Weighting by where people live raises exposure {{claim:exposure.uplift_pct}} per cent above the area mean. An independent national record differs from the field by {{claim:nbro.diff_pct_2021}} and {{claim:nbro.diff_pct_2022}} per cent in two separate years, while three low-cost records sit below it, so the absolute level remains open. The pattern is not supported below the kilometre scale: measured in cities with dense networks, more of the within-city variation lies inside a single cell than between cells, and no freely available covariate places it. Scoring what each information source is worth across {{claim:frame.cities}} monitored cities shows that, in the latitude band Kandy belongs to, a local observation improves daily city-mean accuracy more than a regional background series, while the ordering reverses for detecting exceedances. The measurement that would settle the most is a reference-grade instrument at Kandy, which would fix the level and anchor every low-cost sensor deployed after it.
