The method has three parts, and they are described in the order a reader needs them. The first
is the data, which were restricted from the outset to what any city could obtain. The second is
the model: how a field that must preserve the city-wide mean is split into a regional background
and a local increment, how each part is built, and what constraint bounds the split between them.
The third is the part that cannot be skipped for a city like Kandy, which is how a field can be
checked when the city it describes has almost nothing to check it against. The cross-city
measurement of what each observation is worth, which supplies the measurement priorities, uses the
same construction and is described with its results in {{ref:a-app-panel}}.
