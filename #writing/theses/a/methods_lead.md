The method has three parts, and they are described in the order a reader needs them. The first
is the data, which were restricted from the outset to what any city could obtain. The second is
the model: how a field that must preserve the city-wide mean is split into a regional background
and a local increment, how the temporal anchor, the background and the local pattern are each
built, and what constraint bounds the split between them. The third is the part that cannot be
skipped for a city like Kandy, which is how a field can be checked when the city it describes has
almost nothing to check it against: independent records at Kandy, the complete model in analogue
cities with dense monitoring, and the deployed model placed on a cross-city ladder of information
budgets. The ladder's own design is described in full in {{ref:a-app-panel}}.
