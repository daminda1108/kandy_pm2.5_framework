### Why the limit is one of support, and what stations would add {#s-spatial-bridge}

Three further results explain the limit and test whether a few stations would lift it. They are
summarised here and given in full in {{ref:a-app-spatial}}.

**The variation lives inside the cell.** At Kandy and in the panel cities, the spread of
concentration within a typical one-kilometre cell is larger than the spread between cells across
the whole domain ({{ref:s-reason-change-support}}). A kilometre-scale map cannot represent
variation that occurs inside its cells, however it is built, so the comparison of a cell mean with
a kerbside point is a comparison of different quantities.

**At matched support the model is of the right order.** Averaged over a month, the model's
between-cell contrast is {{claim:field.contrast_monthly}}, close to but below the range measured
between stations of densely monitored cities at the same averaging window, and the contrast is
smaller in the model than in those networks ({{ref:s-matched-support-model-close}}).

**A handful of stations does not supply a map.** A registered test on the densest networks in the
public archives found that, at three to eight stations, neither interpolation nor a free land-cover
or satellite surface ranked held-out neighbourhoods usefully, and that interpolation clearly
overtook the free layer in only {{claim:v2.curve.full.holm_crossing}} of twenty-three cities once
multiple testing was accounted for ({{ref:s-what-stations-buy-map}}). Stations fix the level and the
daily sequence of a city mean, which {{ref:s-validation-ladder}} measures; they do not, on this
evidence, fix a neighbourhood map.
