## 2.12 Three registered tests of the confirmation's robustness

The confirmation measured each observation against one starting estimate, built by one learner from
one set of free streams (Section 2.3). Because every gain is measured against the rung below it
(Section 4.1), a reader can fairly ask whether the confirmed verdicts are properties of the observations
or of that particular starting point, and whether they depend on how many stations each city
contributed. We asked this in three ways after the confirmation was scored. Each
was registered on the Open Science Framework before any result using its new ingredients was computed,
and each is reported against its own rules.

### 2.12.1 A richer sensorless baseline

The first test adds to the bottom rung the free information a practitioner would most likely reach for
next. The rung gained 13 features from five global sources: the surface PM2.5 of the CAMS global
near-real-time forecast, which represents regional transport [@CAMSforecast]; the
tropospheric NO2 column from TROPOMI, as a proxy for changing emissions [@Veefkind2012];
counts of active fires within 100 and 300 km from FIRMS, with a three-day sum [@Giglio2016; @FIRMS_MCD14DL];
daily precipitation from IMERG [@Huffman2023]; the day of the week; and six terrain
descriptors from the Copernicus 30 m elevation model [@CopernicusDEM]. Every feature is taken at,
or around, the city centroid used for the weather drivers, so none depends on where monitors stand.

Three choices were made before the registration and are stated in it. The CAMS system assimilates
satellite observations of aerosol optical depth and trace gases; its documentation lists no surface
PM2.5 monitors among its assimilated data, so it does not carry the monitor information for which we
excluded GHAP (Section 2.1.4). Ocean pixels were excluded from the terrain statistics using the elevation
model's water mask, because a sea stored as 0 m made coastal relief describe the sea. And a city entered
only if CAMS, IMERG and FIRMS covered at least 90 % of its days; applied before registration, this rule
excluded no city.

The frozen estimator then ran twice on one frame: once with the confirmed bottom rung and once with the
richer one, with identical splits, seeds and weights. The first run had to reproduce the registered
confirmation city by city before any result of the second was written. Seven endpoints were registered:
that the richer rung improves on the confirmed one; that the first two stations still help and that
their gain shrinks; and that the confirmed verdicts for stations three to six, the background, the
two-sided ordering and the exceedance ordering survive.

### 2.12.2 Different learners for the bottom rung

The second test replaces only the learner that builds the bottom rung, keeping its features as
confirmed. Three alternatives were fixed in advance. The first is TabPFN, a pretrained tabular
foundation model, version 8.5.0 with its v3 weights, given 10,000 training rows per left-out city
drawn equally from the training cities [@Hollmann2025]. The second is a deep sequence model: a
two-layer gated recurrent unit reading the previous 14 days of the daily features, with the static
geography joined through a dense layer, trained with early stopping on a tenth of the training cities.
The third keeps gradient boosting and adds four features derived from the existing weather drivers: the
ventilation coefficient (boundary-layer height times wind speed), its inverse, a three-day mean of the
inverse, and aerosol optical depth divided by boundary-layer height [@Gassmann2000]. No setting was tuned on these cities. The two deep learners ran on one GPU type in one
environment, and a repeated fit of the first city reproduced each exactly.

Each learner's out-of-city predictions replaced the bottom rung inside the otherwise unchanged
estimator. The confirmed learner, passed through the same replacement path, had to reproduce the
registered confirmation exactly. For each alternative learner we registered the four directional
confirmed verdicts (twelve tests in all), the two-sided ordering, and its skill relative to gradient
boosting, predicting that neither TabPFN nor the recurrent network would improve on it.

### 2.12.3 Full station networks

The confirmation took at most 12 stations per OpenAQ city over the city's last two calendar years
(Section 2.1.2). In a 12-station city that leaves one or two stations to form the background rung, which
carries three of the confirmed verdicts. The third test re-entered every OpenAQ city of both panels with
all members of its cluster, ranked by record length, up to 40, over the city's full driver window. CNEMC
cities already entered with every station and were unchanged. The predictor files were byte-identical to
the confirmation's (checked by SHA-256), and the estimator was the frozen one, re-pointed at the new
station files and nothing else. Before any new record was read, the same code pointed at copies of the
original station files had to reproduce the confirmation city by city; it did, to 3 × 10⁻¹⁴. A station-year
was kept only if every daily file listed for it was retrieved, and an audit of absent days estimated the
remaining loss at no more than 0.04 % in any city.

Six endpoints were registered: the five confirmed verdicts (H1 to H5) on the full networks, and the paired
change from the registered confirmation in the three background endpoints, tested two-sided. A
reproduction check, re-applying the old cap to the newly retrieved records, was registered to be reported
but not used as a gate.
