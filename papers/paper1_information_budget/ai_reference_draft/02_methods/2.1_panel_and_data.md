# 2 Methods

## 2.1 The city panel and its data

### 2.1.1 Drawing the panel

The ladder needs cities where the answer is known: places with enough monitors that some can be
treated as the observations a city might acquire and others held back to score the result. We
therefore drew a panel of well-monitored cities and, in each, simulated the position of a city
that has none.

Candidate cities came from two archives. The OpenAQ archive aggregates government and low-cost
monitoring worldwide [@OpenAQ]; the China National Environmental Monitoring Centre (CNEMC)
network provides dense reference monitoring across Chinese prefectures [@CNEMC].
A city was eligible if it had at least ten stations. Because the question concerns cities in
different climates, the draw was stratified by absolute latitude into four bands: deep tropical
(below 15°), tropical (15° to 23.5°), subtropical (23.5° to 35°) and temperate (above 35°). Each
band had a target of 14 cities. OpenAQ cities were capped at four per country and CNEMC at twelve
cities, so that no single national network could dominate a band. The deep tropics were
enumerated in full because they hold the fewest dense networks (Section 2.1.3); the other bands
were drawn at random with a fixed seed (20260818). [draw_validation_sample.py]

After ingest, 48 cities in 29 countries passed the ingest rules. One, South Africa's only city,
lacks a static-geography stream and is excluded by name (Section 2.4), leaving a discovery frame of
**47 cities in 28 countries** (36 OpenAQ, 11 CNEMC; 13 deep-tropical, 13 tropical, 10 subtropical and
11 temperate), holding 642 stations (8 to 48 per city, median 12) and 30,327 city-days with at least
one valid station-day (median 567 days per city). One further city, a French reference network,
retains only 33 valid days under the 18-hour rule and falls below the 120-day minimum for scoring, so
ladder results cover 46 cities.

### 2.1.2 Observations and quality control

For each OpenAQ city we took up to 12 stations ranked by record length and the most recent two
years of each city's record; OpenAQ records span August 2021 to August 2026 and CNEMC records
January 2022 to December 2025. The cap binds in 45 of the 63 OpenAQ confirmation cities (median 17 stations available) and leaves one
or two stations for the background rung. A registered re-test with each city's full network and full
window (Section 2.12.3) left every confirmed verdict unchanged (Section 3.1.7). Hourly values were kept if they lay strictly between 0 and
1000 µg m⁻³ and were assigned to UTC calendar days. The city's daily concentration is the mean of
all station-hours on that day. Low-cost sensor values were used as reported, without a
city-specific calibration, because the ladder asks what a city would gain from the instruments it
could actually deploy; we return to this in Section 4.4. Each OpenAQ city carries a reference
fraction, the share of its stations flagged by OpenAQ as regulatory monitors, and we classify a
city as reference-dominated when that share is at least one half.
CNEMC cities are regulatory networks and are classed as reference-dominated; 31 of the 47 discovery
cities are reference-dominated and 16 are dominated by low-cost sensors.
A station-day is valid when at least 18 of its 24 hours are present, and daily city and rung values
are equal-weight means over valid stations (Section 2.2).

### 2.1.3 Why the deep tropics are thin, and what that forces

Dense reference networks are rare where the need for a sensorless method is greatest. Querying
the full OpenAQ catalogue of 20,179 locations, we found 6 deep-tropical clusters with at least ten
concurrent reference stations against 65 temperate clusters, a ratio of about 11 to 1
[F.53 as updated 2026-09-04; global_reference_census.py]. Deep-tropical cities in our panel are
consequently dominated by low-cost sensors (69 to 77% of the deep-tropical cell, against about 25%
elsewhere) [F.52, F.107]. Climate band and instrument class are therefore aliased, and the alias
cannot be removed by resampling, because the reference networks that would break it do not exist.
We report band results as describing a regime (a combination of climate and instrument type)
rather than a mechanism of either alone.

### 2.1.4 Predictor streams

Every stream is global and freely available, so that each can be assembled for a city without
monitors.

**Reanalysis drivers.** Seven daily drivers from ERA5-Land [@MunozSabater2021] at the city
centroid: 2 m temperature, the two 10 m wind components, wind speed, boundary-layer height (from
ERA5 hourly at 00, 06, 12 and 18 UTC [@Hersbach2020]), and the sine and cosine of day of year.
Latitude and longitude are deliberately excluded, because a model given coordinates can learn
which city it is predicting.

**Static geography.** Sixty features describing each city's built and natural surface: eleven
gridded layers (vegetation index from MODIS for 2023 to 2024, tree cover from Hansen for 2000,
surface water from the JRC dataset, population, total and non-residential built volume from the
Global Human Settlement Layer for 2020, night-time lights from VIIRS for 2023 to 2024, and four
ESA WorldCover land-cover fractions), each averaged within 100, 300, 1000 and 2400 m; road length
by class (major, medium, minor) within 50 to 1000 m from OpenStreetMap; and distance to the nearest
major road [@Didan2021; @Hansen2013; @Pekel2016; @Schiavina2023; @Pesaresi2023; @Elvidge2017; @Zanaga2022; @OpenStreetMap]. The city value of each feature is its mean over 40 random points
inside the city's **urban centre**, the connected patch of the GHSL degree-of-urbanisation class
"urban centre" (1 km, epoch 2020 [@SchiavinaSMOD2023]) that contains the city centroid,
falling back to the dense-urban and then suburban classes where none exists (one city of 57). A city
without monitors can compute this; an average over monitoring sites, which earlier versions used,
cannot be computed without monitors and describes the monitored neighbourhoods rather than the city:
monitor sites are about 50% denser in population and buildings than their urban centre, and a third
of them are the stations used for scoring [F.116]. Distance to a major road is undefined for one
city with no major road within reach of its points; it is kept as a missing value, which the learner
treats as its own category (effectively "far").

**Satellite level.** Two alternative streams. The primary stream is daily aerosol optical depth at
550 nm from the MODIS MAIAC product (MCD19A2), best-quality retrievals only, averaged within 5 km
of the city centre; days without a retrieval are left missing [@Lyapustin2018]. The
secondary stream is the 2019 to 2022 mean of the GHAP 1 km annual PM2.5 product within 5 km,
a single value per city [@Wei2023]. GHAP is trained on ground monitors that include
the networks in our panel, so it is used only to demonstrate what such contamination does
(Section 3.4); every headline result uses MAIAC.
