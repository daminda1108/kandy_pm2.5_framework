## 2.5 Comparing observations: paired within city

### 2.5.1 Why pairing is required

Cities in the panel differ several-fold in how well the sensorless rung predicts them, and they
differ again in how much any observation can help. When two observation streams are compared by
the median of each stream's gains across cities, the comparison is dominated by which cities score
well on each stream, not by which stream is better within a city. The two medians can even point
in opposite directions from the within-city comparison, a form of Simpson's paradox
[@Simpson1951; @Sprenger2021]. We found this reversal repeatedly in our own
analyses before adopting pairing as a rule (Section 3.5).

Every comparison between two streams, two rungs or two designs is therefore computed within city.
For comparison *A* against *B*,

$$\Delta_c = G^{A}_{c} - G^{B}_{c}, \qquad \hat{\Delta} = \operatorname{median}_c\,\Delta_c,$$

and we report $\hat{\Delta}$ with its bootstrap interval and the number of cities in which
$\Delta_c > 0$. A difference of medians, $\operatorname{median}_c G^{A}_c - \operatorname{median}_c G^{B}_c$,
may be shown to describe the data but is never reported as an effect.

### 2.5.2 Bootstrap over cities

Days within a city are not independent observations of the effect we want; the unit of
replication is the city. Intervals are therefore percentile intervals from resampling cities with
replacement (2,000 to 4,000 draws; 95%). Reporting 28,930 city-days as the sample size would
overstate the evidence by roughly three orders of magnitude.

### 2.5.3 Bootstrap over networks

Cities are not independent either. Many share a national network, operator or instrument
pipeline, and eleven belong to one Chinese network. We therefore also resample in two levels,
first clusters (each OpenAQ country, and the CNEMC network as a single cluster) and then cities
within each drawn cluster, without forcing the resample back to the original size
[@Davison1997; @Field2007]. The 47 scored cities form 28 such clusters,
22 of them holding a single city; the largest is the CNEMC network (11 cities). Under clustering,
the pooled intervals of the three ladder steps widen by a factor of 1.15 to 2.21 across the two
satellite streams. Deep-tropical results are unaffected,
because the 13 deep-tropical cities fall in 12 clusters. We report both intervals for every headline
result and base conclusions on the wider one.

### 2.5.4 Detection limits for null results

A comparison whose interval includes zero is reported with the size of effect it could have
detected. For the spatial tests, the minimum detectable effect is fixed in advance by simulation
(Section 2.8.4). For ladder comparisons, we report the interval itself and describe a comparison
as *undetectable* rather than *absent* when the interval admits a meaningful effect.
