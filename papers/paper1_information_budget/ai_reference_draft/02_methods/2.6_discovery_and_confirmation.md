## 2.6 Discovery and confirmation

### 2.6.1 Why the study is split

Every analysis choice described so far, from the completeness rule to the geography sampling, was
made after looking at the same 47 cities. Some choices corrected defects that the analyses
themselves revealed [F.115]; others replaced designs that proved fragile, such as scoring each
city on a single station split [F.116]. Whatever their merits, choices made after seeing the data
cannot also be tested on those data. We therefore treat everything computed on the 47-city panel as
**exploratory (discovery)** and test the frozen estimator once on cities never used.

### 2.6.2 The freeze

The estimator of Section 2.3 was frozen before any confirmation data were examined: code first at commit
`ba0da66` and, after the order and station-count analyses were added, finally at `e6b744b`, with a
SHA-256 hash of every file in the analysis path (27 files; all re-hashed identical immediately before
scoring). The confirmation endpoints, decision rules, panel and a hash of every predictor file
(383 files) were registered on the Open Science Framework (ueyfr) on 28 September 2026, before any
OpenAQ PM2.5 value of the confirmation cities was retrieved. Hourly data for the 12 CNEMC
confirmation cities were already on disk from an earlier archive census, which the registration
discloses; no ladder or rung analysis had been run on them. The confirmation scorer refuses to read those data until a
local record of the registration exists; its code path was tested beforehand on discovery cities
relabelled as confirmation cities.

### 2.6.3 The confirmation panel

Candidates were every OpenAQ location cluster (single linkage at 25 km within each country) whose
locations were active for at least 365 days between September 2024 and August 2026 and that lay
more than 30 km from every city ever drawn for this study, plus every CNEMC city not used in
discovery, each with at least ten stations. Of about 144 fresh candidates, the discovery caps were
applied (at most four OpenAQ cities per country, twelve CNEMC cities, all tropical candidates taken),
giving **76 cities in 30 countries**, selected from metadata alone with a fixed seed. The panel is
mainly temperate (54) and subtropical (18), with 2 tropical and 2 deep-tropical cities, and 63 of the
76 are dominated by regulatory monitors. It therefore confirms for a mainly mid-latitude, mainly
regulatory population, which we state as its scope.

The fresh supply of dense tropical networks is itself a finding: four cities, against 144 overall.
No confirmation of a tropical-specific claim is possible with public data (Section 4.x).

### 2.6.4 Registered endpoints

In the reconstruction arm, on the confirmation cities only, with two-level cluster intervals:

- **H1.** The first one or two local stations reduce daily city-mean RMSE (lower bound above zero).
- **H2.** Stations three to six add less than one percentage point (interval inside ±1; the bound was
  fixed before the final discovery run and kept when that run's interval came close to it).
- **H3.** A same-network background series reduces RMSE given six stations (lower bound above zero).
- **H4.** The ordering of the background and the first two stations, tested two-sided with no
  direction predicted, because discovery gave no consistent direction.
- **M1.** The advantage of local stations over a background grows toward the equator: the slope on
  absolute latitude in a median regression of the H4 difference on absolute latitude and reference
  fraction jointly. Declared underpowered in advance (four confirmation cities below 23.5°).

- **H5**. For guideline exceedances, a background series is worth more
  than the first two stations: the within-city difference under the exceedance loss (Section 2.9.1)
  has a cluster lower bound above zero. Derived from the discovery panel, where it was the clearest
  ordering found (+34.5 [11.0, 65.9]).

Other secondary analyses (the tail loss, the prospective arm) are reported but do not count toward
confirmation. Any deviation is logged before scoring and reported beside the registered
result. One was: the PM2.5 retrieval was run in three parallel batches of cities rather than one
sequence, using the same frozen function (Section 2.10.2). Before scoring, a completeness audit re-listed
every station-year in the archive and tested a sample of listed but absent daily files for valid PM2.5;
none was found in any of the 64 OpenAQ cities, so the retrieved records are complete.
