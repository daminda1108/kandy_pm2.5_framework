## 2.9 Secondary analyses

### 2.9.1 Which days matter: three losses

Daily RMSE rewards getting the ordinary day right. A city deciding whether to warn the public cares
more about the worst days and about whether the guideline is crossed. Each rung is therefore also
scored by two further losses, with the shrinkage weights still chosen on RMSE so that only the
scoring rule changes:

- **tail loss**: RMSE on the days when the observed city mean is at or above that city's 90th
  percentile;
- **exceedance loss**: one minus the balanced accuracy of predicting whether the daily mean exceeds
  the WHO 24-hour guideline of 15 µg m⁻³ [@WHO2021], defined only for cities with at least five
  days on each side.

Percentage gains are computed exactly as for RMSE (Section 2.2), and orderings are paired within city.

On the discovery panel the ordering of the background series and the first two stations depends on
the loss. On RMSE the two are tied (difference +4.8 [−23.6, +52.1]); on the tail loss the background
is ahead (+27.5 [+2.7, +55.0]) and on the exceedance loss clearly ahead (+34.5 [+11.0, +65.9]), where
the first two stations add essentially nothing (+0.0 [−3.7, +1.5]). The same holds in the prospective
arm (tail +33.3, exceedance +43.2, both intervals above zero). This is registered as a confirmatory
prediction (H5, Section 2.6.4).

### 2.9.2 The order of the rungs

Every gain is marginal at its position on the ladder. Moving the background one step earlier, before
stations three to six, changes it little (+34.4% [15.4, 62.2] after them, +31.8% [13.4, 61.5] before),
while stations three to six gain +0.54 [0.21, 0.80] points without a background and +2.39 [1.49, 3.44]
with one already present; both orderings reach the same final skill (difference −0.01). The
conclusions do not depend on the order [F.116]. A background cannot be placed
before any local station, because its coefficient is estimated against local stations.

### 2.9.3 How many local stations

Sweeping the number of local stations from one to eight, each shrunk toward the sensorless rung: one
station reduces RMSE by 14.5% [8.4, 51.8]; a second adds +0.93 [0.37, 1.50] points, paired within
city; three to eight stations add +1.2 to +1.4 points over one, with no further rise [F.116]. Almost
all of the value of a local network is in its first station; the second adds about a point and a
denser network little more. (The unpaired medians, 14.5% for one station and 21.8% for two, would
suggest a seven-point jump; that is a composition effect across cities, and the paired figure is the
effect.) The version-1 estimator had put the second station at +0.09, because its in-sample shrinkage
could switch a noisy extra station off entirely.

### 2.9.4 An admitted variable that was never used

Daily precipitation was merged into the frame but absent from the feature list. A registered test
added it (OSF z89kt): the sensorless rung did not improve (−1.04% [−5.5, +4.3]) and no gain above it
moved; its verdicts are in Section 2.10 [F.112, F.115].
