# 3 Results

## 3.1 The registered confirmation

We report the confirmation first, because it is the only test in this paper whose endpoints were fixed
before the data existed on our side. Discovery results (Sections 3.2 to 3.5) are exploratory and are
read in its light.

### 3.1.1 The panel as scored

Of the 76 registered cities, 72 were scored. Four were excluded by the registered rules and not
replaced: one had seven stations with data, and three had fewer than 200 days matched to the
reanalysis drivers. The scored panel spans 29 countries plus the CNEMC network, and is mainly
temperate (50 cities; 18 subtropical, 2 tropical, 2 deep-tropical) and mainly regulatory (63 of the 76
registered cities reference-dominated), as the registration stated in advance. The background rung
could be formed in 68 cities; in four, too few stations remained after the held-out and rung stations.
A completeness audit, which re-listed every station-year and tested a sample of listed but absent
daily files, found no file holding a valid PM2.5 value that the ingest had missed.

### 3.1.2 The registered endpoints

Table 3.1 gives each endpoint against its rule. Four of five directional hypotheses were supported; the
moderator was not.

The first two local stations reduce error, as registered, but by **less than half** the discovery
estimate. Four further stations add a quarter of a point, inside the equivalence bound fixed in
advance. A same-network background series adds the most. The ordering between the background and the
first two stations, which the discovery panel could not resolve, is resolved here: in this population
the background is worth more, for the ordinary day (H4) and more clearly for guideline exceedances (H5).

The moderator M1 is not supported. With four cities below 23.5°, the registration declared it
underpowered and fixed in advance that a null would be reported as undetectable; its interval spans
slopes from −1.8 to +1.5 points per degree. Nothing in the confirmation therefore bears on whether the
ordering reverses in the deep tropics, which remains an exploratory hint from discovery (Section 3.4).

### 3.1.3 Secondary endpoints (registered, not counted toward confirmation)

The prospective arm, in which weights and coefficients come from the earlier half of each record and
scoring from the later half, reproduces every verdict (H1 +6.5 [2.5, 23.5]; H3 +44.3 [26.8, 61.6];
H4 +27.3 [5.1, 48.0]; M1 +0.71 [−1.89, +1.62]). Under the tail loss, RMSE on days at or above each
city's 90th percentile, the first two stations add nothing detectable (−2.7 [−10.3, +3.7]), while the
background minus first two is +54.2 [31.0, 66.7]. Under the exceedance loss, stations three to six
change no classification in 74 % of cities, so that effect is exactly zero at the median.

### 3.1.5 The verdicts survive a richer starting estimate

[Registered, OSF b379r; record `kandy_pm25/docs/rich_baseline_results_2026-09-28.md`, ledger F.118.]
Adding CAMS PM2.5, NO2, fires, precipitation, day of week and terrain to the bottom rung (Section
2.12.1) reduced its error by 13.1 % [9.2, 16.2] in the confirmation cities. Every confirmed verdict
held on top of it (Table 3.2). The first two stations still reduced error by 8.9 % [3.6, 15.3].
Contrary to the registered prediction, that gain did not measurably shrink: the paired change was −0.7
[−7.1, +1.7]. The new streams improve the estimate in ways the first local stations do not duplicate. The
background still outranked the first two stations, by 21.7 [3.8, 40.7] on the ordinary day and 45.4
[27.9, 58.3] for exceedances. Its lead narrowed by 2.8 and 6.4 points, and neither change excludes zero.
The base run reproduced the registered confirmation to 3 × 10⁻¹⁴.

### 3.1.6 The verdicts do not depend on the learner

[Registered, OSF jea58; record `kandy_pm25/docs/learner_robustness_results_2026-09-29.md`, ledger F.120.]
With the bottom rung built by TabPFN, by the recurrent network or by gradient boosting with ventilation
features (Section 2.12.2), all twelve directional verdicts held (Table 3.2). None of the alternatives
improved on gradient boosting. TabPFN's bottom rung was 11.1 % worse [−50.9, −1.1], and the recurrent
network (+1.8 [−4.3, +8.9]) and the physics features (+2.0 [−0.3, +4.6]) were indistinguishable from it.
At this sample size, a deep model reading two weeks of history adds nothing a tree model does not already
extract from the day's weather.

One result moved, and it moved as Section 4.1 predicts. Under TabPFN's weaker starting estimate the
first two stations were worth more (+18.6 % against +8.5 %), and the ordinary-day lead of the background
shrank to +8.3 [−5.4, +40.6], no longer resolvable. The exceedance ordering held under every learner
(+52.5 to +59.5). The ordinary-day ranking of background against local stations therefore depends on
how good the sensorless estimate is; the ranking for guideline exceedances does not.

### 3.1.7 The verdicts do not depend on the station cap

[Registered, OSF mhgna; record `kandy_pm25/docs/full_network_ladder_results_2026-10-05.md`, ledger F.122.]
With each OpenAQ city's full network (median 17 stations used, against the cap of 12) and its full window
(Section 2.12.3), 75 confirmation cities were scored, three more than under the cap, and 72 had a
background rung (68 under the cap). Every confirmed verdict held (Table 3.2). The first two stations
reduced error by 7.7 % [3.2, 20.0]. Stations three to six added 0.46 points [0.26, 0.73], inside the
registered bound. The background added 46.0 % [34.3, 63.2], and its lead over the first two stations
widened, to 32.0 points [11.6, 50.5] on the ordinary day and 63.4 [33.5, 71.8] for exceedances.

Paired within city against the registered confirmation, neither the background's gain (+0.5 [−1.5, +3.9])
nor its exceedance lead (−0.2 [−3.3, +6.3]) changed detectably. Its ordinary-day lead over the first two
stations grew by 2.4 points [0.6, 8.9]. Not all of that change comes from the added stations. The old cap,
re-applied to the newly retrieved records, did not reproduce the registered run exactly: the public archive
had since added about 1,300 hourly values inside the scored windows of seven cities, and because the
sensorless rung is fitted across all cities, those values moved every city's effects slightly. Measured
against the re-applied cap, the change in the ordinary-day lead was +3.2 [−0.4, +9.5], which includes zero.
The discovery panel moved the same way (exploratory): the first two stations from 22.4 % to 15.2 %, the
background from 34.2 % to 38.9 %.

The background verdicts were therefore not an artefact of a background built from one or two stations.
With full networks the background is, if anything, worth more.
