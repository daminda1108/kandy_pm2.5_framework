## 3.2 How many stations, and in what order (discovery)

Sections 3.2 to 3.5 report the discovery panel: 46 scored cities, MAIAC satellite stream, ladder v2.
They are exploratory. Where the confirmation tested the same quantity we give both.

### 3.2.1 Saturation

The station-count sweep (Section 2.9) adds local stations one at a time, each combined with the
sensorless estimate by a cross-fitted weight. One station reduces error by 14.5 % [8.4, 51.8]. A
second adds 0.93 points [0.37, 1.50], paired within city. From three to eight stations the gain over
one station stays between 1.2 and 1.4 points and does not grow (Figure 3.x). The ladder's own step from
two to six stations gives the same picture: +0.54 [0.21, 0.80] on discovery and +0.22 [0.12, 0.50] on
confirmation, inside the registered equivalence bound of one point.

A city-mean daily estimate therefore saturates after one or two stations. Additional stations in the
same city describe the same daily signal: on the ordinary day, the stations of a network move together.
[Author: link to Section 2.7. Saturation for the daily city mean says nothing about spatial skill, which
is what the spatial learning curve measures.] How the shrinkage weights are chosen matters for this result (Supplementary Note S3.4).

### 3.2.2 Order

Does the value of a background series depend on how many local stations precede it? We moved the
background rung from after six stations to after two. Its gain was 34.4 [15.4, 62.2] after six and
31.8 [13.4, 61.5] after two; stations three to six added 0.54 [0.21, 0.80] without a background and
2.39 [1.49, 3.44] with one; the two routes reach the same endpoint (difference −0.01 [−0.07, +0.04]).
The ladder's conclusions do not depend on its order. The only order effect is that extra stations are
worth about two points more once a background is present, an effect small enough that it should be
quoted in points, never as a ratio.

### 3.2.3 What the first stations are worth, and why the number moves

On discovery the first two stations reduced error by 21.8 % [10.5, 52.9]; on confirmation by 8.5 %
[3.1, 25.1]. Both exclude zero, and the difference is not a contradiction. The gain of any rung is
measured against the rung below it, so it tracks how weak the sensorless estimate is. On discovery it
ranged from 22 to 41 % across learners (F.116), and it was largest where the sensorless estimate was
weakest: 52.9 % in the 13 deep-tropical discovery cities against 10.5 to 18.1 % in the other bands. The
confirmation panel is 70 % temperate, where the sensorless estimate is strongest, and its temperate
cities gained 6.0 % (median of 50). [EXPLORATORY: the band medians are descriptive; neither the
registration nor the design can separate climate from instrument class (Section 2.1.3).]
