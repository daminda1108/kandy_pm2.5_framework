## 3.3 The ranking depends on the question

Which observation is worth more, a background series or the first two local stations, depends on what
the estimate is for. Table 3.3 gives the paired within-city difference (background gain minus
first-two gain) under three losses.

**Table 3.3.** Background gain minus first-two gain, paired within city; median [two-level cluster
95 %]. Positive values favour the background. RMSE: all days. Tail: RMSE on days at or above each
city's 90th percentile. Exceedance: balanced error of classifying days above 15 µg m⁻³.

| loss | discovery (46 cities, exploratory) | confirmation (72 cities, registered) |
|---|---|---|
| RMSE | +4.8 [−23.6, +52.1] | **+24.6 [+4.1, +47.8]** (H4) |
| tail | +27.5 [+2.7, +55.0] | +54.2 [+31.0, +66.7] (secondary) |
| exceedance | +34.5 [+11.0, +65.9] | **+59.3 [+33.9, +67.7]** (H5) |

The episode losses separate the two observations most clearly. The first two local stations do
almost nothing for high days or for exceedances. On the tail loss they add +7.8 [−3.7, +27.7] on
discovery and −2.7 [−10.3, +3.7] on confirmation, and on the exceedance loss their median effect is
zero on both panels. A background series reduces the tail error by 38 % on discovery and 46 % on
confirmation.

For the ordinary day the answer depends on the panel. On discovery the two observations were
indistinguishable. On confirmation the background was worth more, and the registered two-sided test
resolved an ordering. Section 3.1.4 shows that this ordering does not rest on the CNEMC network.
