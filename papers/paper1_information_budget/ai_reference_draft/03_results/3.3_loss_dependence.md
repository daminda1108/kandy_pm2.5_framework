Under the episode losses the registered first-stations rung does almost nothing: on the tail loss
+7.8 [−3.7, +27.7] on discovery and −2.7 [−10.3, +3.7] on confirmation, and a median of zero on the
exceedance loss on both panels. That is what a recalibration should do. It corrects a city's level and
scale once, so it cannot tell one day from the next, and episodes are exactly where the days differ. The
background rung, read day by day, reduces the tail error by 38 % on discovery and 46 % on confirmation.

When the first two stations are also read day by day, the difference under the exceedance loss is −0.11
[−2.49, 0.00] on the union and 0.00 [−1.56, +0.95] prospectively (Section 3.1.8). The registered ranking
under every loss is therefore a ranking of uses, not of observations. For a city the practical point is
the same for ordinary days and for episodes: a station's value lies in reporting on the day.
