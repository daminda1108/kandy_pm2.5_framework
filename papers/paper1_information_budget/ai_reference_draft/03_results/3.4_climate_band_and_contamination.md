## 3.4 Climate band, and what a monitor-trained covariate does

### 3.4.1 The deep tropics

In the 13 deep-tropical discovery cities the first two stations reduced error by 52.9 % [27.5, 64.4],
and the background series by 15.1 % [5.9, 29.6]. Their paired difference, background minus first two,
was −27.4 [−47.8, +11.8] on the RMSE loss (n = 12). The direction favours local stations, but the
interval includes zero. In the prospective arm the difference shrank to −4.1 [−56.1, +1.3]. Under the
exceedance loss the background led even here: +7.7 [+0.1, +34.3].

An earlier version of this analysis, run on one station split with one learner seed, put the local
stations far ahead in the deep tropics: −33.3 [−50.1, −7.0] on the same scale, or local stations worth
4.2 times the background. Repeated over 20 splits, that interval excluded zero in only 3 (Section 2.3.5). It is one
draw from a wide distribution, and we do not report it as a result.

The confirmation could not test this. Its registered moderator, the slope of the ordering on absolute
latitude, was +0.53 [−1.79, +1.49] per degree with four cities below 23.5° (Section 3.1). Public
archives hold too few dense tropical networks to confirm it (Section 2.1.3). The tropical pattern
therefore remains a hypothesis. In this panel it is also aliased with the dominance of low-cost
sensors.

### 3.4.2 A monitor-trained satellite covariate tilts the ranking

Replacing the MAIAC aerosol stream with GHAP, a PM2.5 product trained on ground monitors that include
networks in our panel, changes the ladder in one direction. Paired within city, the background minus
first-two ordering is 5.5 points larger under GHAP (MAIAC − GHAP: −5.5 [−16.8, −1.5], background favoured
under GHAP in 33 of 45 cities). The background's own gain is also larger under GHAP (−1.5 [−3.8, −0.5]).
The first two stations are worth somewhat less under GHAP (15.1 against 21.8 %). Paired, this
difference is +2.3 [−0.6, +16.9] and excludes neither sign.

A covariate that has already absorbed monitor information therefore does not simply raise every rung.
It lowers the measured value of the first local stations relative to a background series, which is the
comparison a monitorless city most needs to get right. Every headline in this paper uses MAIAC, which
contains no monitor data.

An earlier version of this analysis concluded that GHAP roughly halves the measured value of a local
station. Paired within city and averaged over splits, that difference does not exclude zero (+2.3
[−0.6, +16.9], above). What survives is the tilt of the ordering toward the background.
