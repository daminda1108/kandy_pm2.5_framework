## 3.5 Pooled comparisons point the wrong way

Four times in this study a comparison of medians and the same comparison paired within city
pointed in opposite directions (Table 3.4). In every case the pooled comparison was the more
flattering one. It would have become a headline if we had reported it.

**Table 3.4.** The same comparison computed two ways. Pooled: difference of the two medians across
cities. Paired: median of the within-city difference, with its bootstrap interval and the number of
cities in which the first option won.

| comparison | pooled | paired | wins | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| deliberate vs convenience siting, spatial ρ | +0.114 | −0.044 [−0.095, +0.118] | 19/43 | F.103 |
| EO embeddings vs built-up benchmark, spatial ρ | +0.026 (0.327 vs 0.301) | −0.028 [−0.170, +0.084] | 21/47 | F.111 |
| stations 3–8 in temperate cities, % gain | +12.91 | +0.14 | — | F.102 |
| background vs first two stations, % gain (ladder v1, MAIAC) | +13.5 (37.1 vs 23.6) | +2.3 [−10.9, +21.4] | 24/46 | F.113 |

The mechanism is the same each time. Cities differ far more in baseline skill than the two options
differ from each other. An unpaired median therefore reflects which cities each option happened to do
well on, not what the option does inside a city. All results in this paper are paired within city.
Their intervals come from a bootstrap over cities, and over networks and countries where that is the
primary interval (Section 2.5).
