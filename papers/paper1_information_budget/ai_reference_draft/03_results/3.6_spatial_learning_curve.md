## 3.6 What additional stations buy for the map

The ladder asks what a station adds to a city's daily average. A city that wants a map asks a different
question: how well can it rank its own neighbourhoods from cleanest to dirtiest, and how does that skill
grow with the number of stations? The registered spatial learning curve answers this for the 18
densest networks in the public archives (7 countries; one tropical city). At 7 countries the smallest
effect it can resolve is 0.28 in rank correlation, so only large effects are visible.

### 3.6.1 The curve

Table 3.6 shows the median skill of five estimators as the number of fitting stations grows. Two cautions
apply to reading it. Each column holds only the cities dense enough to reach that number of stations,
so the right-hand columns describe two or three cities. And the verdicts below rest on paired
within-city comparisons, not on these pooled medians.

**Table 3.6.** Median within-city rank correlation between predicted and observed held-out station
means, by number of fitting stations k (primary frame; cities contributing at each k in parentheses).
E1, the free built-up raster, uses no stations and is shown for reference.

| k (cities) | 3 (18) | 5 (18) | 8 (18) | 12 (16) | 18 (9) | 25 (7) | 35 (3) | 50 (3) | 70 (2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| free built-up raster (E1) | 0.18 | 0.18 | 0.18 | 0.18 | 0.22 | 0.23 | 0.18 | 0.18 | 0.20 |
| land-use regression (E2) | 0.11 | 0.12 | 0.18 | 0.22 | 0.33 | 0.33 | 0.34 | 0.37 | 0.39 |
| kriging (E3) | 0.11 | 0.15 | 0.18 | 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.16 | 0.36 | 0.40 | 0.50 |

With three stations, every interpolator ranks a city's sites less well than the free raster. With twelve
to eighteen, regression kriging and land-use regression reach about 0.3. Only the few networks with
thirty-five or more stations reach 0.4 to 0.5. The neural process, trained across cities, stays close to
the raster at every density.

### 3.6.2 Registered verdicts

Seven of eleven testable predictions held. **Cities split** (X2, held): in 11 of 18 cities kriging or
regression kriging overtook the raster at some tested density, five of them already at three stations;
in the other 7 no interpolator overtook it within the densities that exist. Land-use regression
saturated at three stations, within the detection limit of the raster (X3). No estimator exceeded a
city's within-cell ceiling (X4). **Site selection did not matter** (X5): choosing fitting sites to span
the covariates gave the same skill as choosing them at random, consistent with Section 3.7. **One
station informs about 1 km** (X7): beyond that distance a held-out site is predicted no better than by
the city mean. And no deep estimator beat regression kriging by more than the detection limit (X9).

Three predictions were refuted. Inverse-distance weighting did not improve steadily with more stations
(X1). Daily maps were not worse than long-term-average maps for kriging, inverse-distance weighting and
one deep arm (X6). TabPFN given coordinates was worse, not better, than without them (X10; −0.05
[−0.10, −0.02] at twelve stations). One prediction could not be tested: the transformer neural process
failed its registered positive control, so its comparison with the convolutional one (X12) is not
interpreted.

X5's registered statistic is exactly zero for a reason of construction, not because the two siting
designs are identical; Supplementary Note S3.1 explains it and gives the replicate-level comparison
(within ±0.012). Table S2 lists every prediction with its rule and outcome in each frame.

### 3.6.3 The curve on full station records

Given each site's full record (Section 2.7.8), the registered freeze rule found a well-covered year for many
more sites. The primary frame grew from 18 cities in 7 countries (745 sites) to 23 cities in 9 countries
(960 sites), which lowered the detection limit from 0.28 to 0.24. For the first time it holds a
deep-tropical city (Bangkok, 65 sites) beside the tropical Indian one, and eight cities, against six,
entered the low-latitude band arm (Table 3.7).

**Table 3.7.** As Table 3.6, on full station records (primary frame, 23 cities).

| k (cities) | 3 (23) | 5 (23) | 8 (23) | 12 (19) | 18 (12) | 25 (9) | 35 (5) | 50 (3) | 70 (2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| free built-up raster (E1) | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.12 | 0.28 | 0.13 | 0.13 | 0.20 |
| land-use regression (E2) | 0.08 | 0.12 | 0.16 | 0.22 | 0.27 | 0.30 | 0.33 | 0.33 | 0.36 |
| kriging (E3) | 0.10 | 0.12 | 0.17 | 0.11 | 0.15 | 0.19 | 0.31 | 0.31 | 0.43 |
| regression kriging (E5) | 0.09 | 0.13 | 0.17 | 0.21 | 0.26 | 0.29 | 0.33 | 0.33 | 0.39 |

In the larger frame the raster ranks sites less well than in the registered one, and the interpolators
match it at three stations and pass it from five to eight. Six of the seven re-tested verdicts repeated.
Under the registered crossing rule, 15 of 23 cities crossed the raster at some tested density, 8 of them
already at three stations, and 8 never did; Section 3.6.5 shows that this split is mostly within sampling
noise. Land-use regression again saturated at three stations, siting by design again
gained nothing (Section 3.6.5 corrects its interval), one station again informed about a kilometre, inverse-distance weighting again failed to
improve steadily, and daily maps were again not uniformly worse than long-term-average maps.

One verdict changed. X4, that no estimator exceeds a city's within-cell ceiling, was refuted. In London and
Bangkok, stations that share a 1 km cell predict one another worse than chance (−0.28 and −0.57), so the
ceiling falls below zero while the estimators still recover the city-wide ranking. We read this as a
ceiling that does not bound skill where neighbouring sites disagree, not as evidence of structure finer
than a kilometre. In the 70 % sensitivity frame the share of cities crossing the raster fell just below
half (48 %), so X2 did not hold there.

**Tropical arm (exploratory; superseded by the estimated difference in Section 3.6.5).** The eight band-arm cities, in seven countries, carry their own detection
limit of 0.28. No tropical city's kriging curve lay above the envelope of the 21 non-tropical primary
cities at any density. Four lay inside it throughout. Three lay partly or wholly below it: Bangkok at two
of seven densities, Medellín, and one Indian city. On the median curve, a station-based estimator ranked
sites better than the raster from three stations in all seven cities that could be scored.

**Terrain (exploratory, X-T).** Whether hilly or enclosed cities have different curves was tested with
four terrain descriptors (relief, the elevation range of the sites, slope and enclosure) against four curve
outcomes (reach, crossover, kriging skill at twelve stations and its advantage over the raster), sixteen
uncorrected rank correlations in each frame. None of the 32 intervals excluded zero, in the registered frame
(18 cities) or on full records (23 cities); the smallest permutation p was 0.09. The test can resolve only
correlations of about 0.5 or larger, and in the registered frame three of the four enclosed cities are in one
country, so terrain and network are partly aliased. Terrain is undetectable as a moderator at this size, not shown to be
irrelevant.

### 3.6.5 Post-hoc: how much of the curve is sampling noise

[Added 2026-10-06; F.124. Not registered; files `spatial_curve{,_full}/analysis/{x5_erratum,reanalysis,
satellite_benchmark}.json`.]

Four checks from Section 2.7.9 change how strongly the curve can be read.

*The siting interval (erratum).* The registered X5 interval of [0.00, 0.00] was an artefact of pooling over
estimators that do not depend on the sites chosen. Computed for kriging alone, cLHS minus random siting is
+0.013 [−0.062, +0.061] at three stations in the registered frame and 0.000 [−0.049, +0.046] on full
records, −0.024 [−0.042, +0.024] at five. Siting by design still buys nothing resolvable, now with an
interval that means something.

*Do cities really split?* On full records the spread of kriging-minus-raster differences between cities is
not larger than the sampling noise of a rank correlation over a city's sites (Cochran's Q, p = 0.06 to
0.26 across densities; I² 0.23 to 0.42). Only 2 of 23 cities are individually distinguishable from zero,
and with a Holm correction over the densities tested **3 of 23** cities cross the raster (3 of 18 in the
registered frame), against 15 of 23 under the registered first-pass rule. The "split" is mostly noise.

*Tropical against other cities.* Estimated directly, kriging's advantage over the raster at three
stations is lower in the tropical cities by 0.22 on the Fisher-z scale [−0.45, +0.02] (7 against 21
cities). No difference is resolved, but the point estimate favours the raster in the tropics, so "inside
the temperate envelope" overstated the agreement. With the observed spread of per-city differences (SD 0.31
on full records, 0.37 registered) in place of the registered 0.20, the detection limits are **0.35** and
**0.51**, not 0.24 and 0.28.

*A satellite benchmark.* GHAP's annual 1 km surface ranks held-out sites at a median of 0.125 [−0.031,
+0.297] on full records, beside 0.097 for the built-up raster (paired difference +0.018 [−0.143, +0.170]).
Kriging from three, five or eight stations minus GHAP: +0.012, +0.002 and −0.017, each interval spanning
about ±0.15. GHAP may have been trained on these monitors, so this is an upper bound for the free surface.
At the densities a monitor-less city could afford, neither free products nor interpolation rank
neighbourhoods usefully: every median sits near 0.1.

### 3.6.4 What this tells a city like Kandy

The curve gives no station count for a Kandy map. With three to eight stations, no method we tested ranked
neighbourhoods usefully in the typical city, tropical cities could not be told apart from temperate ones,
and the cities where interpolation clearly overtook a free layer were few (3 of 23). Each station informs
roughly the kilometre around it, which is the width of the first distance bin and so an upper bound on
the resolution of that statement. None of the tropical cities is a valley city of Kandy's kind. A Kandy
map from a handful of stations should be expected to carry the city's level and its days, not its
neighbourhoods.
