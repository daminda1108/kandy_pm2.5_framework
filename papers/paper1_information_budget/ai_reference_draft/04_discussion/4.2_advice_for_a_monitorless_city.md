## 4.2 What a city without monitors should measure first

The confirmation supports four pieces of advice for cities like those it covered, which were mostly
temperate and subtropical and mostly served by regulatory networks.

First, use the free streams before buying anything. Reanalysis drivers, a map of the city's surface
and a daily satellite retrieval can be assembled for any city at no cost. Every gain reported here is
measured on top of them, so a city that skips this step will see larger gains from its first
instruments without being better off.

Second, what a station is worth depends mainly on whether its reading is used on the day it is taken.
Read day by day, two stations reduced daily error by about 58 % (+58.8 [44.9, 69.0], 100 cities), and it
made no resolvable difference whether they were the first local stations, a low quantile of the outer
stations or any two others: every paired difference lay within one point, for ordinary days, for
guideline exceedances and prospectively (Section 3.1.8). Used only to recalibrate the free estimate, the
first two stations gave 8.5 % [3.1, 25.1] on the confirmation panel, and nothing on high days. A city
should therefore plan for stations that report continuously and feed the estimate daily; a short
calibration campaign buys a fraction of the value. The registered finding that a background series
"outranked" the first local stations (H4, H5) reflects this difference in use and should not guide a
purchase.

Third, beyond two same-day stations, more stations add little to a daily city-level estimate: on full
networks a background summarising about ten outer stations added 2.5 points [1.1, 3.7] over two stations
read on the day, and stations three to six added 0.22 points to the recalibration. A city that needs more stations needs them for a spatial
purpose (locating hotspots, siting interventions, exposure mapping), and should judge them against that
purpose. For that purpose the evidence is weak (Section 3.6): at three to eight stations neither
interpolation nor any free product ranked neighbourhoods usefully (rank correlation about 0.1), and only
3 of 23 cities showed interpolation overtaking a free land-cover layer after correction for multiple
testing.

In the tropics the advice cannot be specialised. The confirmation held four fresh low-latitude cities
with dense networks, and the discovery hint that local stations lead a background in the deep tropics
(−27.4 [−47.8, +11.8], 12 cities) is a comparison of the registered rungs, so it carries the same
construction as H4 and is not interpreted. Nothing in this study suggests that the value of a same-day
reading differs by climate band. [Author: this is the paragraph that connects to Paper 2 and to Kandy.
