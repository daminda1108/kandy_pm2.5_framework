## 4.4 Limitations

**The registered rungs were not built alike.** The first stations recalibrate the free estimate; the
background is read on the day (Section 2.3.6). Every registered comparison between them (H4, H5 and the
exceedance and tail secondaries) is a comparison of uses. The post-hoc re-analysis that corrects this
(Section 3.1.8) is exploratory, was run after the registered results were known, and covers only cities
with at least eight pool stations (100 of the union). We could not register a fresh test of it, because
almost no dense networks remain unused: 20 Chinese cities with twelve or more stations, two of them
tropical.

**Fixed-effect leakage between neighbouring cities.** The sensorless rung is fitted leaving out one
city, not one network, so same-network neighbours on the same dates stay in training. Refitted leaving out
each city's whole network (post hoc, `review_registered_lono_summary.json`), the sensorless rung is weaker and
every registered gain above it grows a little: on the confirmation cities the first two stations +13.3 %
(registered 8.5 %), the background +42.5 % (41.1 %), H4 +25.9 (24.6). The registered gains are therefore
slightly conservative for a city whose own network is absent from training, which is the monitor-less case.
The like-for-like comparison is unchanged (background − first two −0.21 [−0.55, +0.13], 100 cities).

**Population.** The confirmation covers mainly temperate and subtropical cities (68 of 72 scored)
served mainly by regulatory monitors (63 of 76 registered). Its conclusions extend to tropical cities
and to networks of low-cost sensors only as hypotheses. The 12 CNEMC cities form a single cluster in
the bootstrap and show the largest background effects. Without them, the registered ordering of the background
above the first stations holds, at about half the size (+11.5 [1.4, 33.3]; exploratory), with the same
caveat about construction.

**Climate and instrument are aliased.** Deep-tropical cities in the discovery panel were dominated by
low-cost sensors (69 to 77 % of stations, against about 25 % elsewhere), and no public data can separate
the two. A band difference in this study describes a regime, not a mechanism of climate or of
instrument alone.

**The truth is a network average.** Held-out stations define the city's daily concentration. Where a
network is sited for compliance near roads, the "city" it describes is the roadside city. Local stations
drawn from the same network share that siting, which may flatter them relative to any observation
from outside it.

**The background is internal.** The background rung comes from the city's own network (Section 4.1). An
independent regional station recovers about 71 % of its value, less at longer distance. A background
series bought in practice is therefore worth less than the rung suggests, and the confirmed ranking
could narrow or reverse for a distant regional monitor.

**Low-cost sensors were not calibrated.** We used them as reported, because the ladder asks what a
city gains from instruments it could deploy. A city that calibrates its sensors against a reference
instrument would likely see larger gains from them.

**The size of a gain depends on the starting estimate.** The verdicts survived a richer bottom rung and
three different learners (Section 3.1.5–3.1.6), but the size of the first-station gain moved with the
quality of the bottom rung (8.5 to 18.6 %), and under the weakest one the registered ordinary-day ordering was no longer resolvable. A city whose
sensorless estimate is worse than ours should expect larger gains from its first stations.

**Cost is not modelled.** A value of information becomes a purchase decision only when set against
cost. A regional background may be free to access, or may need a new instrument and an agreement
between agencies. We report what each observation is worth, not what it costs.

**Network size and record length.** The confirmation used at most 12 stations and two years per OpenAQ
city, which left one or two stations to form the background rung in a 12-station city. A registered re-test
with each city's full network left every verdict unchanged and slightly widened the background's lead
(Section 3.1.7), so the cap does not limit the conclusions. The spatial learning curve, first run on one
year per site, was re-run on full records (Section 3.6.3); five more primary cities and the first
deep-tropical one entered, and one of seven verdicts changed. Trends, rare multi-year events and changes in
a network over longer periods are not addressed.
